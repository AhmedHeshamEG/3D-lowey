# Brief → beats → shots

## From a script

1. Split the narration into **beats**: one idea each. A beat ends where the picture should change.
2. For each beat find its **anchor words** — the noun that names the image ("Enigma"), the verb that turns the idea
   ("cracked"), the number that matters ("1941"). The anchor is where the cut, the pop-in or the camera move lands.
3. Pick **the one image** for the beat. Concrete beats literal ("a machine on a desk"); abstract beats get a visual
   metaphor ("nobody could" → a room full of screens, all dark but one).
4. Duration = the beat's spoken length plus a hold (0.3–0.8 s) for the eye to read it.

## The shot list (share it before building)

| # | Words | Shot | Subject | Action | Camera | Look | s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In 1941, a message…" | medium | Army message on the desk | lamp flickers on | push in, lands on "message" | Ink | 3.2 |
| 2 | "People tried for decades. Nobody could." | wide → punch in | Screen 5 | screens pop on "decades", impact on "Nobody" | punch in on "Nobody" | Comic, twos | 4.0 |

One row per shot. Keep the Look consistent within a scene; change it only when the story changes register (a
flashback in Sketch, an action beat in Comic on twos).

## Rules

- One idea per shot. If a shot needs "and", it's two shots.
- Every shot has a subject a viewer can name in one word.
- Wide shots establish; close shots carry emotion and detail. An explainer is mostly medium and close.
- Don't illustrate every word. Silence and holds are allowed.
- Captions don't repeat the voiceover word for word; an overlay adds what the voice doesn't say (a date, a label).

## Without a script ("make a robot discover something")

Write a three-beat micro-story — setup, turn, reaction — and treat it like a script: anchor each beat to an action
instead of a word, and time it on the timeline's markers.
